Interactive mathematical thought experiment

What if a UFO were four-dimensional?

Choose a familiar reported UFO geometry, rotate a hypothetical higher-dimensional object, and compare its projected 3D appearance with an actual 3D cross-section. Switch on a fifth spatial dimension to push the geometry farther.

Important: These are possible mathematical constructions, not reconstructions of actual craft. The same observed 3D shape could come from infinitely many different 4D or 5D objects.

2. What have we actually reversed?

For the tic tac, saucer, and orb, we start with a four-dimensional ellipsoid:

(x/a)² + (y/b)² + (z/c)² + (w/d)² ≤ 1

Choose a,b,c to reproduce the familiar 3D silhouette; d is a freely chosen hidden extent. A true 4D rotation mixes W with visible coordinates. The triangle uses a triangular 3D prism extended along W instead.

Adding V makes a five-dimensional ellipsoid (or prism), introducing one more independent hidden axis. Both projections and cross-sections can be computed in any finite number of dimensions.

3. What would be surprising?

Tic tac: rotating X into W can make its projected length change without bending its higher-dimensional body.

Saucer: rotating Z into W can make a thin disc look much thicker, or reverse that transformation.

Triangle: a 4D extension can project into skewed, compressed, or expanded triangle-like profiles, depending on the rotation.

Orb: a perfect 4D ball remains a ball under every 4D rotation. Rotational change alone would not reveal its hidden dimension!

Physics caveat: These are geometric predictions only. Whether higher-dimensional matter would interact with light, gravity, or sensors this way is unknown.

Mathematical method: sample points in 4D/5D, apply genuine higher-dimensional plane rotations, project to three visible coordinates, then use an ordinary 3D camera to draw the result on a 2D canvas. The intersection view selects points close to W = the chosen slice position (and V = 0 in 5D mode). Finite points and slice thickness create numerical sampling artifacts.
